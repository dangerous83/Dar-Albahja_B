Prologue

البَهجة

Al-Bahja — joy.

In Arabic, al-bahja is not private happiness — it is delight that must be shared. For centuries Marrakech has been called Madīnat al-Bahja, the City of Joy.
Dar Albahja — دار البهجة — means the House of Joy: a promise that the city's joy will be served at your table.

Act I — The City · 01

Marrakech doesn't whisper.
It sings.

Ochre walls that hold the evening light. Souks that smell of cumin, leather and orange blossom. Lanterns that carve constellations into plaster ceilings, and doors — always doors — that promise a garden behind every wall. A brand born here cannot be minimal for minimalism's sake. It must be generous, patterned and warm, like the city itself.

Act I — The City · 03

One silhouette says
“Morocco” at a glance.

The tagine: a shallow dish, a conical lid, and inside — patience. Steam circles until lamb falls from the bone and apricots melt into amber. It is cuisine, ceremony and architecture in one object.

Remember this shape. Before this story ends you will find it hiding in plain sight — eight times over.

06

Act I — The City · 04

Rotate a square,
and a star is born.

Lay one square over another, turned 45 degrees, and you get the khatam — the eight-point star that seeds every zellige wall in the medina. Eight is the rhythm of Moroccan ornament: eight petals, eight gates, eight directions of welcome.

Eight will be the rhythm of this mark.

Act II — The Hand

It began, as always,
with a pencil.

A compass, a rule, a cream sheet of paper. Before a single pixel, the emblem lived as graphite: an eight-fold grid drawn the way the old maâlems drew zellige — from the centre, outward.
